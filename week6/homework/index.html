Reading Responses

Reading 1: Programming Design Systems

Quote 1

“Digital products allow users to interact with their content”

I think this is interesting because there’s a lot more to designing an experience than just how something looks. You also have to think about how people interact with it and how the design responds to them.

Quote 2

“static design tools like Illustrator and Sketch fail at prototyping digital systems.”

I agree with this to an extent because I think static design tools are still really useful, especially in the early stages of a project. But when you’re designing something interactive, there’s only so much you can communicate through a static screen.

Quote 3

“dynamic systems”

I like the idea of designing something that can change rather than having one fixed outcome. It makes me think about how we can create experiences that adapt to different people and situations instead of expecting everyone to interact with something the same way.

Reading 2: What Do Prototypes Prototype?

Quote 1

“identifying the most important open design questions”

I think this is something I could be better about in my own design process. Sometimes I jump into making prototypes without really thinking about what I want to learn from them, and I end up spending time on details that aren’t necessarily important yet.

Quote 2

“very simple representations make highly effective prototypes”

This is something I’ve noticed in my own projects, especially when doing user testing. Sometimes the simplest prototypes give you the most useful feedback because people focus more on how something works rather than how finished it looks.

Quote 3

“Prototypes themselves do not necessarily communicate their purpose.”

I hadn’t really thought about this before, but it makes sense that someone looking at a prototype might not understand what part of the design is actually being tested. It reminds me how important it is to be clear about what I’m trying to learn, especially when asking people for feedback.
